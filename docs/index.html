How to read this

This scores projects on public evidence, not companies on character. Every scored signal has a linked public source and a capture date. No evidence found means the signal scores zero, logged as no public evidence found — that is not a claim the thing doesn't exist, only that it wasn't found in the public record.

  • Site control — 25 Recorded ownership/lease with a traceable paper trail; a government counterparty (city, county, university) is the strongest version.
  • Physical commitment — 25* A docket-numbered TCEQ air permit tied to the specific site — the single strongest "this is real" signal. See the three meanings below.
  • Financial commitment — 15 An executed interconnection or power-supply agreement, a PUC filing, or an SEC-disclosed financing figure specific to the project.
  • Incentive filings — 15 An executed Texas JETI Act (Ch. 403) agreement or a county/municipal tax abatement agreement earns up to the full 15. A match on the Comptroller's separate §151.359 "Qualifying Data Center" registry caps at 7 of 15.
  • Sponsor track record — 20 A documented pattern of the sponsor delivering announced projects before, at claimed scale, on roughly claimed timelines — specific cited instances, not general reputation.

*Three meanings of a missing Physical Commitment permit — treating them identically would be the single biggest distortion in this dataset:

  • Confirmed, permitted (score 5–25) — a TCEQ air permit, a TDLR building permit, or equivalent was found and tied to the site.
  • True zero (score 0) — the project's own stated power strategy would require an individually-permitted facility, and no such permit was found despite that being the reasonable expectation. Shown as "0 (true zero)".
  • Not observable by category (excluded from scoring, not scored as zero) — the project's power strategy is exactly the profile Texas's "permit by rule" regime (30 TAC §106.511) was built to wave through without individual public notice. A missing permit here tells you nothing. Shown as "n/o (§106.511)", and the total is renormalized over the remaining four signals (÷ 75 × 100) rather than defaulted to zero.

Reversals are flagged, never subtracted. Where a project has a publicly documented, dated setback since its original announcement (a cancelled tenant-funding pact, a terminated anchor lease, an open lawsuit), no points are subtracted beyond what the underlying evidence already implies. The reversal is logged as a flag with a citation in the evidence file — a fact to report, not a hit to double-count.

Method Corrections Substack

Assay takes no money from the developers, sponsors or projects it scores. It uses public records only and publishes results whether they pass or fail.
