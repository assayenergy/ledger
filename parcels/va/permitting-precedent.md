## Part 2 — neighboring counties with similar ordinance language

- **Isle of Wight County** (adjoins Surry): its single-tier, exclusion-based "Utility scale solar
  energy facility" definition (Sec. 2-1002 — *"a disturbance zone... [greater than] two (2)
  acres... not mounted on a building... not integrated PV only"*) is **structurally near-identical
  to Southampton's** definition of "utility scale solar energy project." The decisive difference:
  **Isle of Wight's ordinance imposes no minimum-MW floor anywhere** for that category — unlike
  Southampton's Sec. 18-624(1), which sets a 20 MW minimum for the same single tier. Isle of Wight
  has permitted a 3 MW project (Courthouse Hwy Solar 1) under this exact framework by CUP, with no
  amendment needed. `[gov-record]`
- **Prince George County** (adjoins Sussex and Surry): its ordinance (Sec. 90-1) has **only two
  tiers, split purely by acreage** — "small" (<10 acres, nominally capped at "100 MW," an
  internally meaningless ceiling) and "large-scale" (≥10 acres, no MW language at all). Prince
  George approved **Powell Creek Solar, a 5 MW / 47-acre project, as a "large-scale solar energy
  facility"** by Special Exception in A-1 (Sec. 90-53(59)), unanimously, Aug. 9, 2022 — despite the
  acreage-only test producing a mismatched label (a modest-MW project called "large-scale" purely
  because its footprint exceeds 10 acres). This is the same kind of label/reality mismatch already
  documented in this ledger for Sussex, Surry, and Southampton, and it did not block approval.
  `[gov-record]`
- **Greensville County** (adjoins Southampton and Sussex): unlike Prince George, Greensville's
  ordinance layers an MW-based definition of "utility-scale" (>1 MW, generally >2 acres, §
  23-2-125.3) **on top of** a separate siting rule requiring any individual utility-scale facility
  to occupy **at least 100 acres** (§ 24-3(k)) — with "medium-scale" capped at 999 kW / 3 acres (§
  23-2-125.2) below that. This leaves the same kind of dead zone Southampton has: nothing in the
  ordinance accommodates a project sized like the Phase II awards (2–5 MW, tens of acres). **No
  project at or below 5 MW has been approved in Greensville** under this framework — every
  confirmed Greensville solar approval found is 49–86 MW. `[gov-record]`/`[press]` This is a
  negative precedent: where the Southampton-style gap exists *and* an MW floor or acreage minimum
  is actually enforced, small projects do not get approved.

## What this implies for Sussex, Surry, Southampton

**Every statement in this section is an INFERENCE. None of these are findings. None of these
should be repeated to a landowner, a developer, or in publication as a statement of what would
happen in these counties — they describe what similar ordinance language did elsewhere, not what
Sussex, Surry, or Southampton's Boards of Supervisors would do.**

- **Sussex** — INFERENCE: Sussex's own gap (a "solar facility, shared" definition with no row in
  the permitted-use table, Sec. 16-403(c)) is structurally different from any single case
  researched, but Prince George's Powell Creek Solar is the closest available analog: an
  ordinance whose internal size-test arithmetic doesn't cleanly fit a small project, approved
  anyway by treating it as the nearest available tier (there, "large-scale" by acreage; here,
  presumably "medium-scale" or "utility-scale" by staff discretion). **This suggests, but does not
  establish,** that Sussex's Zoning Administrator (Beverly Walkup) could plausibly process a ≤5 MW
  shared-solar CUP application by analogy to an adjacent tier — but no Sussex application at this
  scale has ever tested the question (confirmed separately in this ledger's Sussex "why no award"
  research), so this remains untested.
- **Surry** — INFERENCE: Surry already has a clean, named "community-scale" tier (>1–≤5 MW)
  requiring CUP in M-1/M-2, so it does not share the CUP-eligibility gap the other two counties
  have. Its open question is narrower and different in kind: whether the 7%/10,695-acre cap
  (which names only "utility-scale" in its operative sentence) also binds community-scale
  projects. **None of the nine cases researched resolve an analogous cap-scope question** —
  Greensville's cap language names "utility scale solar facilities" only, but Greensville has no
  separate named middle tier for the ambiguity to attach to the way Surry does. This suggests the
  precedent record simply doesn't speak to Surry's specific ambiguity; it remains open, and only
  Surry's own Planning Director (Horace H. Wade III) or a future BOS interpretation could resolve
  it — not analogy to another county.
- **Southampton** — INFERENCE, but the strongest-supported inference in this document: Southampton
  shares its exact single-tier, exclusion-based "utility scale" definition structure with **both**
  Isle of Wight and Shenandoah — and both of those counties successfully permitted ≤5 MW projects
  (3 MW and 5 MW respectively) under that identical structure, specifically **because neither also
  imposes Southampton's 20 MW minimum floor** (Sec. 18-624(1)) on the same category. This suggests
  the 20 MW floor — not the exclusion-based definition itself — is the anomalous element causing
  Southampton's internal contradiction, since two structurally-matched counties operate without it
  and without incident. It does **not** establish what Southampton's own Board would actually do
  with a real application: Greensville shows that where a similar gap exists *and* is actually
  enforced, small projects get blocked outright, not waived around. Southampton's Director of
  Community Development and Planning (Regan Prince) is the only source who could resolve which
  outcome Southampton would produce — no project at or below 20 MW has ever tested it there
  (Southampton's only built facility is the 100 MW Southampton Solar; the one project in process,
  BAP Power/Cenergy, is 20 MW, at the floor, not below it).

repeated as a finding, and must never support a statement made to a landowner, a developer, or in
publication. Every "implies" statement above is tagged **INFERENCE** for this reason and should
