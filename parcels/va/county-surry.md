# Surry County, VA — Solar & Fiscal Profile

> Disclosure: the author has a financial interest in land acquisition in the counties covered by
> this research.

As of 2026-09-22. Every fact is tagged `[gov-record]` (a county ordinance, Board of Supervisors
record, or SCC/state filing) or `[press]` (news reporting). Where a fact could not be sourced, it
says so explicitly and names the document that would settle it.

## Solar ordinance

- **Original adoption:** December 6, 2018 (originally placed in Chapter 10, Environment; later
  recodified into the Zoning Ordinance). `[gov-record]` — recited in the [Feb. 13, 2025 Public
  Hearing Notice](https://surrycountyva.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1861/Public-Hearing-Notice---Board-of-Supervisors---Solar-Ordinance---21325).
- **Most recent amendment:** Board of Supervisors held a public hearing and voted **3–1 on
  February 13, 2025** to adopt PC Ordinance Amendment 2024-02 — recodifying the solar rules into
  the Zoning Ordinance (Appendix A) and tightening the acreage cap (see below). `[gov-record]`/`[press]`
  — draft ordinance (above); ["Surry supervisors vote 3-1 to enact 7% cap on solar
  farms," Smithfield Times, Feb. 19, 2025](https://www.smithfieldtimes.com/2025/02/19/surry-supervisors-vote-3-1-to-enact-7-cap-on-solar-farms/).
- **By-right vs. SUP/CUP:** **Never by-right**, and **not permitted at all in the Agricultural-
  Rural (A-R) district.** Utility-scale solar is permitted only by **Conditional Use Permit** in
  the **M-1 (Light/General Industrial)** and **M-2 (General Industrial)** districts — a project on
  A-R land must first obtain a Comprehensive Plan amendment and rezoning to M-1/M-2, then a CUP.
  §§ 3-902(C), 3-1002(C). `[gov-record]`
- **Setback from residences:** **300 feet** from all abutting public rights-of-way and main
  buildings on adjoining parcels/property lines (§ 4-608(A)(7)), paired with a matching 300-foot
  opaque vegetative buffer (§ 4-608(A)(13)(a)). `[gov-record]`
- **Acreage/percentage cap:** **7% of "developable land" within the county, or 10,695 acres
  total**, per § 1-501.1 — quoted directly: *"the total approved utility-scale solar generation
  facilities shall not exceed 7 percent of developable land within the County or 10,695 acres in
  total."* This **replaced a looser 10%/15,278-acre cap** adopted in 2023. `[gov-record]`/`[press]`
  — draft ordinance § 1-501.1 (above); ["Surry County approves 10% cap on solar farms," Smithfield
  Times, April 10, 2023](https://www.smithfieldtimes.com/2023/04/10/surry-county-approves-10-cap-on-solar-farms/); ["Surry proposing stricter 7% cap, down from 10%," Smithfield Times, Oct. 30, 2024](https://www.smithfieldtimes.com/2024/10/30/surry-proposing-stricter-7-cap-on-solar-farms-down-from-10/).
- **Inter-project spacing rule:** **No hard-number minimum distance.** Proximity to other large-
  scale solar facilities is only a discretionary factor the Board may weigh when considering a
  waiver of standards on a CUP (§ 4-608(B)(4)) — not an independent numeric requirement.
  `[gov-record]`

Other ordinance provisions of note: facility centroid must be within one mile of existing
high-voltage transmission lines; a county-developer siting agreement is required; max height 18
ft; decommissioning bond = 100% of estimated cost + 20%. `[gov-record]` — §§ 1-501.1, 4-608(A).

## Does this apply at 5 MW

**AMBIGUOUS** (on the acreage cap specifically; APPLIES on CUP and setback).

Surry's ordinance (Article II, Sec. 2-202, Use type descriptions) defines three size tiers,
quoted verbatim: `[gov-record]`

- *Distributed solar energy facility*: "An solar energy generation facility with a rated capacity
  less than [1] MWac. Distributed solar energy facilities are used to meet energy demands on-site
  and include rooftop and groundmounted photovoltaic arrays on residential, commercial, and
  institutional properties." (Treated as an accessory use — Sec. 4-204.A.4.)
- *Community-scale solar energy facility*: "An energy generation with a rated capacity greater
  than 1 MWac (megawatts alternating current) and no greater than 5 MWac. Facilities do not
  require connections to high voltage transmission lines."
- *Utility-scale solar energy facility*: "A solar energy generation facility with a rated capacity
  greater than 5 MWac."

**A project rated at exactly 5 MW AC falls at the top edge of "community-scale," not
"utility-scale"** (which requires *greater than* 5 MWac).

- **CUP requirement: APPLIES.** The M-1 and M-2 permitted-use tables list "Community-Scale Solar
  Energy Facility*" as its own distinct row, allowed only by Conditional Use Permit — the same
  requirement, and the same M-1/M-2-only district restriction, as utility-scale. §§ 3-902(C),
  3-1002(C). `[gov-record]`
- **300-ft setback: APPLIES.** Sec. 4-608 is explicitly titled *"Community-Scale Solar Energy
  Facility or Utility-Scale Solar Energy Facility"* and opens: *"Performance standards for
  community-scale solar energy facilities and utility-scale solar energy facilities"* — the
  setback, buffer, decommissioning, and all other §4-608 performance standards apply to both
  tiers identically. `[gov-record]`
- **7%/10,695-acre cap: AMBIGUOUS.** Sec. 1-501.1's heading also names both tiers together
  ("Conditional use permits for community-scale solar energy facilities or utility-scale solar
  energy facilities"), but the operative cap sentence itself uses only the narrower term: *"the
  total approved **utility-scale** solar generation facilities shall not exceed 7 percent of
  developable land within the County or 10,695 acres in total."* Surry's ordinance is otherwise
  careful to write "community-scale... or utility-scale..." every time it means to cover both (the
  §4-608 title itself, and its decommissioning subsections 2–3). The narrower wording in the cap
  sentence specifically is plausibly deliberate — meaning a 5 MW community-scale facility may
  require a CUP without counting against the 10,695-acre ceiling — but this reading has not been
  confirmed by any official interpretation, letter, or BOS record found. `[gov-record]`

  **This remains unresolved even after identifying and pulling the approval record for Cavalier
  Solar** (the 240 MW facility adjoining parcel P5, documented in full in
  [surry-adjacent-facility.md](surry-adjacent-facility.md)) — **stated plainly, not stretched: that
  record does not touch this ambiguity at all.** Cavalier is unambiguously utility-scale at 240 MW
  under any definition, old or new, so it says nothing about how a 5 MW *community-scale* project
  would be treated. More importantly, Cavalier was approved in **May 2021**, under Surry's original
  undifferentiated Dec. 2018 solar ordinance (a single "solar farms" category, no MW tiers at all)
  — nearly four years **before** the community-scale/utility-scale split existed as ordinance text
  (Feb. 2025). A precedent that predates the very distinction in question cannot resolve it. The
  only size-category language in Cavalier's record — the siting agreement's own phrase, "a utility
  scale energy facility" — confirms Surry's utility-scale pathway works as described; it is not
  evidence about community-scale at all. `[gov-record]`
- **Inter-project spacing: N/A for either tier.** No numeric spacing rule exists; proximity to
  other facilities is only a discretionary factor the Board may weigh when granting a waiver for a
  *utility-scale* CUP specifically (Sec. 1-501.1(B)(4)) — not an independent requirement, and not
  extended to community-scale by its own terms. `[gov-record]`

**Official whose interpretation would settle the cap ambiguity:** Horace H. Wade III, Director,
Surry County Department of Planning & Community Development — (757) 294-5210.

**Pending applications:** none found in Surry's public record as of this research — no pending
CUP application for a solar facility appears in Surry's Agenda Center listings or in press
coverage searched. **Document that would confirm this:** Surry County Planning & Community
Development's current CUP application log.

**The arithmetic, and why it doesn't resolve cleanly:**

- Summing the acreage figures above (Cavalier's 1,341 acres now `[gov-record]`-confirmed; the
  other four still at their earlier confidence levels):
  2,950 + 672 + 1,341 + 1,650 + 124 = **6,737 acres used**, implying **3,958 acres of headroom**
  (~37% of the 10,695-acre cap remaining).
- This still conflicts with Planning Director Wade's October 2024 press-quoted estimate that
  existing approvals already used "roughly 6% of developable acreage" (`[press]`, Smithfield
  Times, Dec. 19, 2024, above). Since the 7% cap equals 10,695 acres, "developable land" implies
  ≈152,786 acres total, and "roughly 6%" implies **≈9,167 acres already used** — about **2,430
  acres more** than the bottom-up sum above, and implying only **≈1,528 acres of headroom** (~14%
  of the cap), not the ~3,958 acres (~37%) the per-project sum suggests. Confirming one of the
  five inputs precisely (Cavalier) narrowed the gap only slightly (from ~2,471 to ~2,430 acres) —
  it did not close it, because the other four projects' acreage remains the larger source of
  uncertainty.

These two approaches disagree by roughly 2,500 acres and cannot be reconciled with the sources
available in this research. **I am not confirming either figure as the remaining headroom.**

report and site plan, which this research could not read directly.

## Land use taxation

**No.** Surry County has not adopted a land use value assessment ordinance under Va. Code §
58.1-3230 et seq. Virginia Tech's LUVA program lists Surry explicitly: "Surry — No use-value
assessment ordinance." `[gov-record]` — [luva.aaec.vt.edu/estimates](https://luva.aaec.vt.edu/estimates/).
Corroborated by the absence of a land-use-taxation article in Surry's own Code of Ordinances,
Chapter 16 (which has only property-tax *exemption* provisions for the elderly/disabled and farm
equipment). `[gov-record]` Since there is no program, there is no enrollment deadline.

## Why no Phase II award here

Dominion's Phase II shared solar awards (all dated 7/1/2026) include Smithfield, Suffolk,
Petersburg, Chesapeake, Norfolk, Toano, and King William — none in Surry County, despite Surry
having more existing utility-scale solar capacity than any of the other two counties in this set.
`[gov-record]` — [Dominion Shared Solar Program page](https://www.dominionenergy.com/virginia/renewable-energy-programs/shared-solar-program).

**No public-record explanation found stating a cause.** What was checked:

- Dominion's program page states only "Capacity will be awarded on a first-come basis" — no
  locality-based allocation, quota, or exclusion process is described anywhere in the program
  rules. `[gov-record]`
- No local news coverage (Smithfield Times and others searched) discusses Surry's absence from
  the Phase II award list specifically.
- SCC's shared-solar program overview page references docket **PUR-2024-00122**, but that page
  carries no locality-level detail; an interactive query at
  [scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch](https://scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch) was not completed for
  this specific question.

**One factor worth flagging as context, not a confirmed cause — and now weaker than previously
stated:** a shared-solar facility (state-capped at 5 MW) would be classified under Surry's
ordinance as a "community-scale solar energy facility," still requiring a CUP in M-1/M-2 zoning
only (see "Does this apply at 5 MW" above). Whether it would also count toward the 7%/10,695-acre
cap is itself **ambiguous** on the ordinance's own text (the cap sentence names only
"utility-scale," not community-scale). And the acreage-headroom picture is now **UNVERIFIED**, not
"nearly exhausted" — see "Cap status — Question 2" above, where Planning Director Wade's ~6%
estimate and a bottom-up per-project sum disagree by roughly 2,500 acres. Given both the cap's
applicability to this size tier and the actual remaining headroom are unresolved, this can no
longer be offered as a plausible explanation with any confidence — **it is flagged here only as a
factor that would need to be resolved before ruling it in or out**, not as a likely cause.
