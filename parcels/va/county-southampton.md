## Solar ordinance

Article XXII, "Utility Scale Solar Project for Southampton County," County Code Ch. 18 (Zoning),
§§ 18-621–18-635. Verified directly against the Planning Commission's final draft sent to the
Board of Supervisors after its March 10, 2022 meeting. `[gov-record]` — [draft ordinance text](https://cms7files1.revize.com/southamptoncountyva/Final%20draft%20after%20Mar%2010%20PC%20meeting.pdf).

- **Adoption timeline:** A first utility-scale solar ordinance was adopted **December 2018**.
  `[press]` — ["Southampton County supervisors approve new solar ordinance," Tidewater News,
  Sept. 10, 2022](https://www.thetidewaternews.com/2022/09/10/southampton-county-supervisors-approve-new-solar-ordinance/).
  The Board adopted a **12-month moratorium on new utility-scale solar applications, Sept. 10,
  2021 – Sept. 10, 2022** (4–3 vote, Sept. 9, 2021) to rewrite the ordinance. `[press]` —
  ["Southampton County board establishes solar moratorium," Tidewater News, Sept. 10,
  2021](https://www.thetidewaternews.com/2021/09/10/southampton-county-board-establishes-solar-moratorium/).
  **Most recent/current ordinance: public hearing and unanimous Board approval July 26, 2022**,
  replacing the 2018–2019 rules; applications resumed after the moratorium expired. `[press]` —
  same Sept. 10, 2022 Tidewater News article.
- **By-right vs. SUP/CUP:** **Not by-right.** Utility-scale solar is permitted only in the **M-1,
  Limited Industrial** zoning district, requiring a zoning map amendment and/or Conditional Use
  Permit — land zoned agricultural must first be rezoned to M-1. Sec. 18-625. `[gov-record]`
- **Setback from residences:** New substations/connector stations set back **≥500 feet**;
  inverters/transformers set back **≥250 feet** — both from existing dwellings, schools, places
  of worship, State Scenic rivers, and rights-of-way. Sec. 18-627(2). A separate buffer
  requirement (half the setback, minimum **100 feet** where the adjacent parcel has a residence,
  place of worship, historic structure, cemetery, or school) applies within the setback. Sec.
  18-629(1). `[gov-record]`
- **Project size:** Minimum **20 MW**, maximum **100 MW** nameplate capacity per project. Sec.
  18-624(1)–(2). `[gov-record]`
- **Acreage/percentage cap:** Total land devoted to utility-scale solar countywide **shall not
  exceed 1% of Southampton County's land area**, with farm/forest ratios tied to Virginia Farm
  Service Agency and Dept. of Forestry data. Sec. 18-624(3). `[gov-record]` Reported separately
  (staff figure, not in the ordinance text) as **3,855 acres** in absolute terms, of which 69%
  must remain forested. `[press]` — Sept. 10, 2022 Tidewater News article (above).
- **Inter-project spacing rule:** **No utility-scale solar project may be sited within 5 miles of
  another such project**, measured at the nearest exterior boundary of each. Sec. 18-630(8).
  `[gov-record]`

## Does this apply at 5 MW

**AMBIGUOUS.**

Southampton's ordinance does **not** define separate small/medium/community-scale tiers. It
defines only one category, "utility scale solar energy project," and does so by **exclusion**, not
by a floor on MW size (Sec. 18-622): `[gov-record]`

> *"Utility scale solar energy project, solar energy project or project means a renewable energy
> project that either: (a) Generates electricity from sunlight, consisting of one or more PV
> systems... or (b) Utilizes sunlight as an energy source to heat or cool buildings... and (c) Does
> not meet any of the following criteria: has a disturbance zone equal to or less than two acres,
> is mounted on or over a building or parking lot or other previously disturbed area, or utilizes
> integrated PV only."*

By this text, **any** ground-mounted solar project disturbing more than 2 acres — regardless of MW
— is defined as a "utility scale solar energy project" subject to the whole of Article XXII. But
Sec. 18-624(1) then sets a **20 MW minimum** nameplate capacity for "any utility scale solar
energy project" under this same article: `[gov-record]`

> *"The minimum rated nameplate capacity of any utility scale solar energy project shall be at
> least twenty (20) megawatts (MW)."*

**A 5 MW AC / ~30–40 acre project is caught in a direct textual contradiction**: its disturbance
zone (~30–40 acres) is far above the 2-acre exclusion threshold, so it qualifies as a "utility
scale solar energy project" by definition — but it then fails that same category's own 20 MW
floor. No smaller tier, exemption, or by-right path exists in the ordinance for anything above 2
acres of disturbance and below 20 MW. Southampton's own staff have acknowledged this exact gap:
at the August 5, 2021 joint Board of Supervisors/Planning Commission workshop, staff stated the
ordinance "doesn't speak to anything less than 5 megawatts, except it says you're a utility-scale
solar development if you disturb more than 2 acres of land." `[gov-record]` — [BOS Aug. 5, 2021
minutes](https://www.southamptoncounty.org/BOS%20Minutes/2021/BOSMINUTES08052021.pdf).

Because the project's basic categorization under the ordinance is unresolved, whether the 1%/
3,855-acre cap (Sec. 18-624(3)) and the 5-mile spacing rule (Sec. 18-630(8)) — both written to
apply to "utility scale solar energy projects" — would attach to it is likewise **AMBIGUOUS**, not
a clean APPLIES or EXEMPT.

**Official whose interpretation would settle it:** Regan Prince, Director of Community
Development and Planning, Southampton County — (757) 742-6756, rprince@southamptoncounty.org.
`[gov-record]` — [Southampton County Community Development](https://www.southamptoncounty.org/departments/community_development/index.php).

## Cap status

As of the **July 26, 2022** ordinance-adoption hearing, county staff (Community Development
Director Beth Lewis) stated the 1%/3,855-acre cap had **1,418 acres already used** by the
existing Southampton Solar project, leaving **2,437 acres available**. `[press]` — Sept. 10, 2022
Tidewater News article (above). **No subsequent public record found stating the cap has since
been reached or exceeded** — only one additional project (BAP Power/Cenergy, 20 MW / 71 acres,
see below) has advanced since 2022, which alone would not exhaust the remaining ~2,437 acres.
**Document that would settle current status:** a current Southampton County Community
Development staff memo or GIS tally of cumulative acreage under CUP.

## Why no Phase II award here

Dominion's Phase II shared solar awards (all dated 7/1/2026) include Smithfield, Suffolk,
Petersburg, Chesapeake, Norfolk, Toano, and King William — none in Southampton County, despite
Southampton having existing utility-scale solar. `[gov-record]` — [Dominion Shared Solar Program
page](https://www.dominionenergy.com/virginia/renewable-energy-programs/shared-solar-program).

**No public explanation found.** What was checked:

- Dominion's program page states only "Capacity will be awarded on a first-come basis" — no
  locality-based allocation or exclusion process is described. `[gov-record]`
- **Acreage cap: not a plausible blocking factor on current evidence.** As of July 2022, roughly
  2,437 of 3,855 capped acres remained unused (see "Cap status" above), and shared-solar
  facilities are capped at 5,000 kWac (≈25–40 acres) — far smaller than the remaining headroom.
- **Moratorium/ordinance timing: ruled out.** The county's 2021–2022 moratorium predates the
  Phase II legislation (2026) by several years.
- **A structural friction point worth flagging as a plausible-but-unconfirmed factor:** county
  staff noted at the Aug. 2021 workshop that the ordinance "doesn't speak to anything less than 5
  megawatts, except it says you're a utility-scale solar development if you disturb more than 2
  acres of land" — meaning a small shared-solar-scale (≤5 MW) project could still trigger the same
  CUP/rezoning-to-M-1 process as a 100 MW project. `[gov-record]` — [BOS Aug. 5, 2021 minutes](https://www.southamptoncounty.org/BOS%20Minutes/2021/BOSMINUTES08052021.pdf).
  No project record was found confirming or refuting that this deterred any specific Subscriber
  Organization — **this is a plausible mechanism, not a documented cause.**
- No document found stating a substation/distribution capacity constraint specific to
  Southampton, and no document found either confirming or denying that any Subscriber
  Organization applied for Phase II capacity sited in the county.
