## 2. Authoritative source for territory boundaries

**Statutory basis:**

- Va. Code **§ 56-265.3** ("Certificate to furnish public utility service; allotment of
  territory...") — the original mechanism: any utility furnishing service as of July 1, 1950 was
  entitled to a Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity for its territory upon filing
  **maps with the Commission showing the territory then being served** (a late-filing window ran
  through January 1, 1974). These original filed maps, plus every subsequent SCC order amending,
  transferring, or resolving a dispute over them, are what actually define a certificated
  boundary today — not any modern summary map. `[gov-record]` —
  [law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title56/chapter10.1/section56-265.3](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title56/chapter10.1/section56-265.3/).
- Va. Code **§ 56-580** (Virginia Electric Utility Regulation Act) — confirms incumbent electric
  utilities continue to provide distribution service within their exclusive SCC-established
  territories under this framework. `[gov-record]`

**Case-level record:** territorial boundaries between neighboring utilities/co-ops are litigated
and resolved as individual SCC cases when disputed. Example directly on point: **Case No.
PUE-1996-00295**, a dispute between Prince George Electric Cooperative and Dominion (then Virginia
Electric and Power Company) over which utility should serve a customer whose site sat within
PGEC's certificated territory. `[gov-record]`/`[press]`-cited case reference; reported at 1998
S.C.C. Ann. Rep. 344. Cases like this — searchable by party name or territory keyword at
[scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch](https://scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch) — are the authoritative
record for any specific boundary that has ever been contested.

**Published map (starting reference only, not the legal record):** the SCC's own statewide
"Electric Service Territories" map, created by its Division of Public Utility Regulation, carries
this exact disclaimer printed on the map itself: *"This is an approximation, please contact the
Division of Public Utility Regulation for official electric territory maps."* `[gov-record]` —
[el_map.pdf](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/media/sccvirginiagov-home/consumer-home/public-utilities/electricity/regulated-companies-amp-service-map/el_map.pdf).
**This map is useful only to identify which utilities are plausibly in contention for a county —
never to confirm a specific parcel.**

**Where to go for an official answer:** the SCC's Division of Public Utility Regulation (the
office that created the above map and processes § 56-265.3 filings and amendments) is the
authoritative contact for a specific-parcel boundary question, reachable through the SCC's general
Division of Public Utility Regulation contact channels via [scc.virginia.gov](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/regulated-industries/public-taxation/public-service-corporations/).

## 3. Repeatable parcel-level determination procedure, ranked by reliability

**Tier 1 — confirms a determination on its own:**

1. **The parcel's own utility account.** If the site is an existing structure or an already-built
   solar/energy facility, the utility actually billing that meter is dispositive. For a proposed
   project, the applicant's own interconnection application or CUP narrative typically **names the
   interconnecting utility explicitly** (e.g., "the Project will interconnect with Dominion Energy
   Virginia's distribution system," or the equivalent naming a cooperative) — this is the same
   standard already used elsewhere in this ledger for gridscore's Texas projects, and should be
   applied the same way here.
2. **An SCC case or order specifically naming that site, parcel, or immediate area** (a territorial
   dispute resolution, a certificate amendment, or a transfer order) — searchable at
   [scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch](https://scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch). This is the single
   strongest form of evidence because it is the SCC's own adjudicated record for that exact
   location, not an inference from a map.
3. **Direct written confirmation from the SCC's Division of Public Utility Regulation**, or from
   the utility/cooperative itself, identifying the specific parcel or address.

**Tier 2 — strong supporting evidence, but not confirmation on its own:**

4. **The cooperative's own member/outage-lookup tool**, if one exists and can be queried by
   address (co-ops typically maintain an interactive outage map or "find my co-op" tool tied to
   their actual metered service points — this reflects real accounts, not a drawn boundary, but
   should still be corroborated with Tier 1 where the determination matters).
5. **Dominion's own service-territory or hosting-capacity tools** (e.g.,
   [dominionenergy.com/hostingcapacity](https://www.dominionenergy.com/hostingcapacity)), for the
   same reason — reflects Dominion's own operational footprint, not a legal boundary per se.

**Tier 3 — starting point only; never sufficient for a determination:**

6. **County-level "which utilities serve this county" lists** (a cooperative's own marketing
   "About Us" page, this document's Section 1 table above, or similar) — useful only to identify
   which utilities are *plausibly* in contention for a parcel in that county. Ruling a utility
   *in* at the county level never rules others *out* at the parcel level, and should never be read
   as evidence for a specific parcel.
7. **The SCC's own statewide approximate map** (Section 2, above) — explicitly self-disclaimed as
   an approximation. Useful for the same narrowing purpose as #6, nothing more.

## 4. Which parts of each county are patchwork

**All three counties should be treated as patchwork in full — none supports a safe county-level
default.** This is a direct consequence of Section 1: every one of the three counties has at
least two confirmed electric providers (Dominion plus at least one cooperative), and Sussex and
Southampton each additionally border or contain a wholly separate municipal utility.

- **Sussex County**: Dominion and Prince George Electric Cooperative are both confirmed present
  county-wide (not just at the edges) — PGEC is headquartered in Waverly, inside Sussex. The
  incorporated **Town of Wakefield** operates its own municipal electric utility, a third,
  entirely distinct provider for any parcel within town limits. Southside Electric Cooperative's
  reach into Sussex (reported, unconfirmed — see Section 1) would add a fourth possibility along
  the county's western edge toward Dinwiddie if confirmed. **No sub-area of Sussex is safe to
  assume Dominion (or any single provider) without a parcel-level check.**
- **Surry County**: Dominion, Prince George Electric Cooperative, and Community Electric
  Cooperative are all confirmed present. Community's territory is concentrated toward Surry's
  eastern side (nearer Suffolk/Isle of Wight), while PGEC's is broader across the county —
  **but this is a directional tendency from the utilities' own marketing descriptions, not a
  confirmed line, and should not be treated as a sub-county safe zone.**
- **Southampton County**: Dominion, Prince George Electric Cooperative, and Community Electric
  Cooperative are all confirmed present, with Community's territory concentrated in the rural
  parts of the county (per its own description) alongside Suffolk and Isle of Wight. Southampton
  also wraps around the independent **City of Franklin**, which operates its own municipal
  electric utility — any parcel near or within Franklin's boundary needs a check against that
  separate, non-jurisdictional provider as well as the three above.

## 5. Replacing the P(Dominion) probability column with a three-state determination

The prior pipeline used a **P(Dominion)** column — a probability estimate, presumably derived from
county-level base rates or similar heuristics. **A probability is not a determination**: it
encodes uncertainty about which utility serves a parcel without ever resolving it, and it invites
treating a merely-plausible utility as a confirmed fact once a threshold is crossed. Given Section
1's finding that every county in this set has multiple confirmed providers, any county-level base
rate would misclassify a meaningful share of parcels no matter where the threshold is drawn.

**Replace `P(Dominion)` with a single column, `utility_determination`, restricted to exactly three
values:**

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CONFIRMED_DOMINION` | Dominion Energy Virginia is confirmed as the interconnecting/serving utility for this specific parcel, by Tier 1 evidence (Section 3). |
| `CONFIRMED_COOP` | A specific electric cooperative is confirmed as the interconnecting/serving utility for this specific parcel, by Tier 1 evidence. Record **which** cooperative (PGEC, Community, or Southside) in a companion free-text field — `utility_determination` alone does not distinguish between them, and that distinction matters (e.g., for the low-income/shared-solar program questions elsewhere in this ledger, which are Dominion-specific). |
| `UNDETERMINED` | Tier 1 evidence has not been obtained. This is the default and starting state for every parcel in Sussex, Surry, or Southampton given the patchwork finding in Section 4 — it is not an error state, and it is expected to be the majority value until parcels are individually checked. |

**What moves a parcel to `CONFIRMED_DOMINION` (or `CONFIRMED_COOP`):** only Tier 1 evidence from
Section 3 — the parcel's own utility account or the project's own interconnection filing naming
the utility, a specific SCC case or order naming that site, or direct written confirmation from
the SCC's Division of Public Utility Regulation or the utility itself. Tier 2 or Tier 3 evidence
(a cooperative's own general outage-map tool, Dominion's hosting-capacity map, a county-level
"who serves this county" list, or the SCC's disclaimed statewide map) is **never sufficient by
itself** to set `CONFIRMED_DOMINION` or `CONFIRMED_COOP` — a parcel supported only by that kind of
evidence stays `UNDETERMINED`, however suggestive the evidence is. Downgrading a row that is
already `UNDETERMINED` requires no action; upgrading it requires citing the specific Tier 1
document or contact in an accompanying evidence/source field, consistent with the tagging
convention (`[gov-record]`/`[press]`) used throughout this ledger.
