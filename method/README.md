# /method

How the scoring works, in plain English, plus the unabridged methodology documents.

- **[gridscore-method.md](gridscore-method.md)** — GridScore scores announced data center
  projects on five public-record signals: site control (25 pts), physical commitment (25 pts),
  financial commitment (15 pts), incentive filings (15 pts), and sponsor track record (20 pts).
  Every point requires a linked source; signals that genuinely can't be observed in the public
  record (like backup generation covered by Texas's permit-by-rule exemption) are excluded from
  scoring entirely rather than counted as zero, and the total is renormalized over the remaining
  signals when that happens. Projects land in one of three tiers — Evidenced (70–100),
  Progressing (40–69), or Announced-only (0–39) — and any documented reversal (a cancelled
  tenant, a failed financing) is flagged with a citation but never subtracted from the score.
  Full detail, including every judgment call: [assayenergy.substack.com](https://assayenergy.substack.com/).

- **[flexvalue-method.md](flexvalue-method.md)** — FlexValue estimates what grid flexibility is
  worth at a given ERCOT pricing node: for a hypothetical 1 MW load, how much cheaper is
  electricity if it can curtail itself during expensive hours versus running flat, 24/7. It
  models three flexibility tiers (by notice required and max curtailment hours) across three
  value components — energy avoidance, ECRS ancillary revenue, and 4CP transmission-charge
  avoidance — and expresses the result as a $/MWh and percentage discount versus firm load.
  Full detail: [assayenergy.substack.com](https://assayenergy.substack.com/).
